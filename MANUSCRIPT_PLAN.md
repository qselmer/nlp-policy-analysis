# Manuscript plan

## Working title

**Managing small pelagic fisheries in highly variable upwelling systems under climate change: a systematic evidence synthesis and decision-support framework**

Alternative titles:

1. **From static rules to climate-adaptive management: evidence for small pelagic fisheries in highly variable upwelling systems**
2. **Management under environmental non-stationarity: a systematic synthesis for small pelagic fisheries and the Peruvian anchoveta**
3. **Which management responses are ready for use? Evidence maturity and transferability for climate-exposed small pelagic fisheries**

## Recommended article identity

The paper should be presented as a **systematic evidence synthesis with an applied decision-support framework**, not as a purely narrative legal review and not as proof that the identified measures are effective.

The central contribution is the explicit separation of:

- proposed, recommended, planned, piloted, implemented, and evaluated measures;
- source quality and legal authority;
- operational specificity and data feasibility;
- transferability to the north-central Peruvian anchoveta;
- expert acceptance and analytical robustness;
- implementation and demonstrated effectiveness.

## Main research question

**Which management measures have been implemented, evaluated, recommended, or proposed for small pelagic fisheries exposed to high environmental variability and climate change, what evidence supports them, and under what conditions could they inform climate-adaptive management of the north-central Peruvian anchoveta stock?**

## Secondary questions

1. Which environmental and climate pressures are linked to ecological and fishery responses in the reviewed evidence?
2. Which management measures are reported, and at what stage of implementation maturity?
3. How strong, independent, and geographically distributed is the evidence supporting each measure?
4. Which measures are ecologically and operationally transferable to the Peruvian anchoveta fishery?
5. Which options remain stable under alternative evidence filters and leave-one-source-out analysis?
6. Which evidence gaps prevent direct operational use?

## Recommended structure

### 1. Introduction

#### 1.1 Small pelagic fisheries in highly variable upwelling systems

Establish why short-lived small pelagics are particularly exposed to environmental non-stationarity through changes in recruitment, growth, condition, distribution, availability, and trophic interactions.

#### 1.2 The management problem

Explain the mismatch between dynamic ecological systems and management frameworks that often rely on fixed seasonal procedures, historical productivity assumptions, or weakly specified environmental responses.

#### 1.3 What is missing from existing reviews

State that prior literature discusses climate impacts, adaptation principles, or individual case studies, but rarely separates:

- proposals from implemented measures;
- implementation from evaluated performance;
- number of findings from independent evidence;
- general relevance from operational transferability.

#### 1.4 The Peruvian anchoveta as a transferability case

Present anchoveta as a high-value application in a strongly variable Eastern Boundary Upwelling System. Avoid making the paper exclusively Peruvian; the international synthesis must remain the primary contribution.

#### 1.5 Objectives and contribution

End with the main question, secondary questions, and three contributions:

1. a systematic map of management responses;
2. an evidence-maturity and robustness assessment;
3. a conditional decision-support framework for anchoveta.

### 2. Methods

#### 2.1 Review design and reporting standard

Describe the study as a systematic evidence synthesis and map. Follow ROSES reporting standards and provide a flow diagram, protocol information, search dates, and version identifiers.

#### 2.2 Eligibility framework

Define population/system, exposure, management intervention, evidence type, geography, language, period, and eligible source types.

#### 2.3 Search strategy

Report databases, institutional repositories, legal sources, grey-literature searches, citation chasing, languages, full search strings, search dates, and update procedures.

#### 2.4 Record management and deduplication

Describe import, metadata harmonisation, DOI/title matching, fuzzy duplicate detection, and retention of source provenance.

#### 2.5 Screening

Report title/abstract and full-text screening, decision categories, exclusion reasons, reviewer roles, reconciliation, and any model-assisted prioritisation.

#### 2.6 Structured finding extraction

Define the finding as the analytical unit and specify the coding of:

- environmental or climate pressure;
- ecological response;
- fishery consequence;
- management measure;
- evidence statement type;
- implementation stage;
- outcome or effectiveness evidence;
- geographic and fishery context;
- document locator and supporting passage.

#### 2.7 Source-quality appraisal

Use source-type-specific appraisal criteria. Do not combine legal authority, methodological quality, and relevance into one undifferentiated score.

#### 2.8 Transferability to Peruvian anchoveta

Describe ecological similarity, climate relevance, management relevance, evidence strength, operational specificity, and Peruvian data feasibility.

#### 2.9 Decision-support synthesis

Explain construction of pressure–response–fishery–management chains, management portfolios, readiness classes, and research priorities.

#### 2.10 Robustness analysis

Report sensitivity scenarios, high-quality and high-transferability filters, observational and implementation filters, leave-one-source-out analysis, source concentration, and stability classes.

#### 2.11 Human validation and reproducibility

Describe expert review, accepted/corrected/rejected statuses, preservation of audit trails, software tests, configuration files, and repository availability.

### 3. Results

#### 3.1 Evidence-flow and corpus characteristics

Report records identified, deduplicated, screened, included, and excluded. Characterise source type, region, system, species group, language, and publication period.

#### 3.2 Environmental pressures and ecological responses

Summarise the main pressure–response pathways, clearly distinguishing observed responses from projections.

#### 3.3 Management measures and implementation maturity

Present each measure by proposed, recommended, planned, piloted, implemented, and evaluated status. The main denominator should be independent sources, not the number of extracted findings.

#### 3.4 Source quality and evidence independence

Report quality ratings, independent-source counts, geographic concentration, institutional concentration, and evaluated implementations.

#### 3.5 Transferability to anchoveta

Present ecological and operational transferability, proposed uses, constraints, adaptation requirements, and evidence gaps.

#### 3.6 Operational readiness and decision pathways

Report which options are candidates for monitoring, bounded pilots, MSE/scenario evaluation, research development, or conditional operational integration.

#### 3.7 Robustness and source dependence

Present scenario-retention rates, leave-one-source-out retention, source concentration, and final stability classes.

#### 3.8 Priority evidence gaps

Identify missing implementation evaluations, missing climate links, weak Peruvian data feasibility, and measures supported by only one independent source.

### 4. Discussion

#### 4.1 Main synthesis

State what the evidence supports and, equally important, what it does not support. Avoid converting recurrent recommendations into demonstrated effectiveness.

#### 4.2 Why environmental variability requires adaptive but bounded management

Discuss non-stationarity, rapid biological responses, monitoring frequency, decision latency, and the need for predefined but revisable response procedures.

#### 4.3 From generic principles to operational measures

Organise the evidence around operational components such as:

- climate-risk assessment;
- environmental monitoring and early warning;
- ecosystem and climate-informed reference points;
- harvest-control-rule testing;
- dynamic or seasonal spatial measures;
- bycatch and juvenile-protection measures;
- MSE and scenario testing;
- institutional review and learning mechanisms.

#### 4.4 Transferability to the Peruvian anchoveta

Use the existing legal and institutional analysis as an applied subsection. Frame identified legal gaps as a case-specific diagnosis informed by the global synthesis, not as the sole basis for the global conclusions.

#### 4.5 Proposed climate-adaptive decision architecture

Present a bounded framework:

```text
monitor → diagnose → classify risk → evaluate response options →
apply authorised measure → monitor outcomes → review and revise
```

Any environmental trigger should be treated as a candidate for testing, not as an automatically validated legal threshold.

#### 4.6 Implications for ecosystem-based fisheries management

Explain how climate information, stock condition, spatial organisation, trophic context, fleet behaviour, and institutional capacity can be integrated without replacing conventional stock assessment.

#### 4.7 Limitations

Address database coverage, grey-literature accessibility, language bias, source dependence, heterogeneity of evidence, publication bias, reliance on qualitative findings, limited evaluated implementation, transferability uncertainty, and the role of expert judgement.

#### 4.8 Research and implementation agenda

Prioritise independent replication, retrospective evaluation, pilot design, MSE, threshold testing, data-governance requirements, institutional authority, and monitoring performance metrics.

### 5. Conclusions

Use four concise conclusions:

1. Management literature contains many proposed and recommended responses but fewer evaluated implementations.
2. Evidence maturity and independence vary substantially among management measures.
3. Transferability to anchoveta is conditional on local ecological, data, operational, and legal requirements.
4. Climate-adaptive management should be introduced through monitored pilots, scenario testing, explicit review rules, and evaluation rather than assumed effectiveness.

## Journal positioning

### Primary target: Fish and Fisheries

Use this target only after broadening the evidence base beyond a small set of sources and strengthening the general conceptual contribution. The paper must be a deep synthesis with broad geographic or taxonomic relevance, not a descriptive scoping review.

### Strong alternative: Marine Policy

Best fit when the paper gives greater weight to governance, institutional design, legal-operational gaps, and policy transfer to Peru. Technical workflow details should remain in supplementary material and the main text should foreground policy implications.

### Alternative review journal: Reviews in Fish Biology and Fisheries

Suitable for an integrated review centred on fish and fisheries biology, assessment, exploitation, conservation, and management, with the anchoveta case used to demonstrate application.

### Alternative interdisciplinary outlet: ICES Journal of Marine Science

Suitable if the manuscript places stronger emphasis on the scientific basis for advice, management under non-stationarity, integrated marine systems, and a broadly relevant climate-adaptive framework.

## Revision of the existing anchoveta manuscript

The current manuscript should not simply receive an additional systematic-review section. It should be restructured so that:

- the global evidence synthesis becomes the main analytical backbone;
- the Peruvian legal analysis becomes a transferability and implementation case study;
- international cases are coded and appraised rather than described selectively;
- management proposals are explicitly labelled as proposals unless implementation is documented;
- effectiveness claims are restricted to evaluated outcomes;
- proposed quotas, thresholds, closures, and committee powers are framed as testable options subject to MSE, legal analysis, monitoring, and evaluation.

This redesign changes the paper from a primarily qualitative normative essay into a reproducible, internationally relevant evidence synthesis with a transparent applied contribution.
